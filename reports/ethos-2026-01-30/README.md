## Core research tension

Ethos presents a substantially stronger operating profile than the first benchmark issuer: rapid growth, GAAP profitability, positive recent operating cash flow, an asset-light balance sheet, and no direct insurance-policy balance-sheet risk.

But the IPO itself has weaker alignment characteristics:

- selling stockholders offer more shares than the company;
- about 59% of company net proceeds are assigned to RSU-related tax withholding;
- public Class A investors receive one vote while Class B receives 20 votes;
- founders, Accel, and Sequoia retain overwhelming voting influence;
- carrier concentration and estimate-sensitive revenue recognition remain material;
- an internal-control material weakness has not yet been fully remediated.

## Positive signals

### Rapid profitable growth

Revenue increased from $159.754m in 2023 to $254.926m in 2024, approximately **59.6%**. Revenue for the first nine months of 2025 was $278m, approximately **47.9%** above the comparable period.

Net income was $48.832m in 2024 and $46.590m for the first nine months of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA was $57.536m and $63.191m for those periods.

### Asset-light risk model

Ethos provides the technology, distribution, underwriting administration, and policy administration. Insurance carriers assume the underlying insurance-policy balance-sheet risk.

### Strong liquidity

Pro forma adjusted cash, cash equivalents, and investments were approximately $198.315m. The model contains no large conventional debt burden, although the calculation treats $27.813m of commission-receivable financing liabilities as debt-like.

## Red flags

### Carrier concentration

The top three carriers represented approximately **98% of total revenue** in both 2023 and 2024. In the first nine months of 2025, the three largest carrier customers represented 39%, 34%, and 14% of revenue.

### Estimate-sensitive revenue

Revenue depends partly on persistency estimates for policy renewals. Updated lapse, carrier, channel, and cohort experience can increase or reduce revenue recognized for prior periods.

This is not automatically poor accounting. It means revenue quality must be evaluated together with cash conversion, estimate changes, and carrier concentration.

### Profitability and cash conversion diverge

Despite $48.832m of GAAP net income in 2024, operating cash flow was negative $10.908m as commissions receivable and other working-capital assets expanded. Operating cash flow improved to positive $31.281m for the first nine months of 2025.

### Existing-holder liquidity dominates

```text
Primary shares      5,127,696
Secondary shares    5,398,619
                    ---------
Total offered      10,526,315
```

Ethos does not receive proceeds from secondary sales. The secondary portion is approximately **51.29%** of the total offering.

### Dual-class control

Class A carries one vote per share and Class B carries 20 votes per share. The co-founders represent about 38.5% of voting power, while Accel- and Sequoia-affiliated entities represent about 57.0%.

Class B includes a time-based sunset no later than the fiscal year following the tenth anniversary of the IPO, subject to earlier ownership and founder-service triggers. Until conversion, minority Class A influence is limited.

### Material weakness

Ethos disclosed a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting. Remediation measures exist, but the company states that sustained effectiveness must be demonstrated before the weakness is fully remediated.

| Lens | ITG | Ethos |

The contrast shows why the framework separates business quality from offering quality. Ethos is the stronger operating business, but its secondary selling, proceeds allocation, dual-class control, concentration, and internal-control weakness prevent a high IPO-quality score.
