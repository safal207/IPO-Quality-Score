# Reviewer Scorecard v0.1

Use this scorecard during human review. It supplements the methodology; it does not replace evidence or analyst judgment.

## Scoring anchors

For each dimension, choose an anchor and explain deviations.

### Revenue growth and durability — 15

- **13–15:** strong, repeatable growth; diversified customers; high recurring quality.
- **9–12:** healthy growth with one or two material durability risks.
- **5–8:** uneven, acquisition-dependent, concentrated, or slowing growth.
- **0–4:** contraction, severe concentration, or unreliable disclosure.

### Profitability and cash flow — 15

- **13–15:** durable profitability and cash generation with high-quality reconciliation.
- **9–12:** credible path or improving economics with manageable adjustments.
- **5–8:** persistent losses, weak cash conversion, or heavy stock compensation.
- **0–4:** deteriorating economics, severe burn, or misleading adjustments.

### Market and competitive position — 10

- **9–10:** strong differentiation, defensibility, and evidence-backed market position.
- **6–8:** attractive market with credible but contestable advantages.
- **3–5:** crowded market, weak switching costs, or uncertain demand.
- **0–2:** structurally weak position or unsupported market claims.

### Valuation quality — 15

- **13–15:** conservative relative to evidence and peers, with modest assumptions.
- **9–12:** reasonable but dependent on continued execution.
- **5–8:** demanding assumptions or weak peer comparability.
- **0–4:** extreme, calculation-blocked, or unsupported valuation.

### Debt and balance-sheet resilience — 10

- **9–10:** strong liquidity, low refinancing risk, and transparent liabilities.
- **6–8:** manageable leverage with identifiable risks.
- **3–5:** material debt, maturity, covenant, or liquidity pressure.
- **0–2:** severe solvency or refinancing concern.

### Use of proceeds — 10

- **9–10:** specific, measurable, growth-oriented primary capital use.
- **6–8:** mostly productive use with some general or secondary component.
- **3–5:** vague use, substantial insider liquidity, or mostly debt repair.
- **0–2:** offering primarily transfers value away from new shareholders.

### Insider selling, lock-up, and dilution — 10

- **9–10:** low secondary selling, strong lock-up, limited overhang, aligned holders.
- **6–8:** moderate dilution or selling with acceptable alignment.
- **3–5:** high selling, broad exceptions, large option or warrant overhang.
- **0–2:** severe misalignment or imminent dilution risk.

### Governance and shareholder rights — 5

- **5:** independent oversight and fair shareholder rights.
- **3–4:** some concentration or defensive provisions.
- **1–2:** strong founder or sponsor control and weak minority rights.
- **0:** extreme governance concern or disclosure failure.

### Legal and regulatory risk — 5

- **5:** limited material exposure and clear compliance disclosure.
- **3–4:** manageable sector or jurisdiction risk.
- **1–2:** material litigation, licensing, privacy, sanctions, or regulatory exposure.
- **0:** existential or unresolved legal risk.

### Disclosure quality and consistency — 5

- **5:** clear, internally consistent, traceable disclosures.
- **3–4:** generally useful with limited ambiguity or management-defined metrics.
- **1–2:** repeated inconsistencies, omissions, or difficult reconciliation.
- **0:** materially unreliable or contradictory disclosure.

## Reviewer rule

Anchor ranges are guidance, not automatic formulas. Every assigned score must include evidence IDs, confidence, and a judgment explaining why the issuer belongs in that range.
