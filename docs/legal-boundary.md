# Legal and Product Boundary

## Scope

IPO Quality Score is designed as a general research, publishing, and educational framework. It organizes publicly available information and transparent analytical judgments for a broad audience.

This document is a product boundary, not legal advice. A qualified lawyer must review the production service in every jurisdiction where it is offered.

## The product may provide

- public IPO calendars and issuer profiles;
- standardized research reports;
- factual summaries of regulatory filings;
- transparent scoring methodology;
- valuation scenarios with disclosed assumptions;
- red-flag and evidence-quality alerts;
- historical comparisons and post-IPO tracking;
- general educational content;
- non-personalized watchlists selected by the user.

## The product must not provide without appropriate authorization

- personalized recommendations based on a user's income, assets, age, objectives, or risk tolerance;
- instructions stating how much a specific person should invest;
- discretionary portfolio management;
- automatic trade execution;
- guaranteed returns or claims that a score predicts profit;
- hidden paid promotion presented as independent research;
- issuer-sponsored ranking changes;
- selective access to material non-public information;
- tax or legal advice represented as professional advice.

## Required communication principles

All public research should be:

- fair and not misleading;
- clear about uncertainty;
- transparent about methodology and conflicts;
- explicit about paid relationships or affiliate compensation;
- separated from advertising;
- dated and versioned;
- corrected publicly when material errors are found.

## Suggested report disclaimer

> This report is provided for general informational and educational purposes. It does not consider any reader's financial situation, objectives, or risk tolerance and is not a personalized recommendation to buy, sell, subscribe to, or hold any security. IPOs involve substantial risk, including loss of capital. Scores describe the framework's assessment of disclosed business, valuation, governance, dilution, legal, and evidence-quality factors; they do not predict investment returns. Verify current offering documents and consult appropriately licensed professionals before making financial decisions.

## Conflicts of interest

Every report should disclose whether the publisher, reviewer, or related party:

- owns or may trade the security;
- received compensation from the issuer, underwriter, broker, or affiliate;
- has a referral or affiliate relationship;
- received early access, data, or other consideration;
- has another material relationship that could affect independence.

When a conflict exists, it must be displayed prominently rather than buried in general terms.

## Sponsored content

Sponsored issuer content must:

- be labeled before the user reads the analysis;
- remain visually distinct from independent scoring;
- never purchase a higher score;
- disclose the payer and type of compensation;
- follow the same factual correction policy.

## Subscription boundary

A subscription may charge for access to:

- faster aggregation;
- deeper standardized analysis;
- evidence-linked reports;
- alerts and version changes;
- research tools and data exports;
- educational group sessions.

Payment must not be framed as purchasing guaranteed profitable signals.

## Jurisdiction checklist before launch

Before accepting customers, obtain jurisdiction-specific review for:

- investment-adviser and financial-promotion rules;
- publisher and research exceptions;
- consumer-protection and advertising rules;
- data privacy and retention;
- terms of service and limitation of liability;
- affiliate and sponsored-content disclosure;
- sanctions and country restrictions;
- tax and invoicing obligations.

## Product enforcement

The production system should block publication when:

- the disclaimer is missing;
- conflicts are undisclosed;
- content contains personalized allocation language;
- a report promises or implies guaranteed performance;
- the exact filing state cannot be identified;
- material facts lack source evidence.
